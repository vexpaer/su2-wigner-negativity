Universal Wigner Negativity for Pure Spin States via $\pi$-Rotation Orthogonality

Ximing Wang (vexpaer@gmail.com) • November 26, 2026 • DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24563264

We prove that for every spin $j \in \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}$ ($j \ge \frac{1}{2}$), every pure state $|\psi\rangle \in \mathbb{C}^{2j+1}$, and every $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-covariant self-dual Stratonovich–Weyl kernel on the sphere $S^2$, the spherical Wigner function $W_\psi(\mathbf{n})$ strictly attains negative values. This settles the open conjecture of Davis et al. (2021, 2023) and completes Hudson's theorem across all two-dimensional constant-curvature phase spaces. The entire proof is machine-verified in Lean 4 with zero sorry and standard axioms only.

01 — Main Result

Completing the Constant-Curvature Trichotomy

Together with Hudson's theorem on the flat plane and recent results for the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ discrete series on the hyperboloid, pure-state Wigner positivity is now completely classified across all three geometries of constant Gaussian curvature $\kappa$.

Main Theorem (Universal Spherical Wigner Negativity)

For every spin $j \in \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}$ ($j \ge \frac{1}{2}$), every unit pure state $|\psi\rangle \in \mathbb{C}^{2j+1}$, and every self-dual $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-covariant Stratonovich–Weyl kernel $\Delta_j^{(\boldsymbol{\epsilon})}(\mathbf{n})$ (all $2^{2j}$ sign choices $\epsilon_l \in \{\pm 1\}$), there exists a unit axis $\mathbf{u}_* \in S^2$ with $\langle\psi|e^{-i\pi\mathbf{u}_*\cdot\mathbf{J}}|\psi\rangle = 0$, and consequently $\min_{\mathbf{n}\in S^2} W_\psi(\mathbf{n}) < 0$ and $\delta(\psi) := \frac{1}{2}\big(\int_{S^2}|W_\psi|\,d\mu - 1\big) > 0$.

03 — Proof Architecture

Three Elementary Steps, All Spins Simultaneously

The proof requires no algebraic geometry of Majorana stars and applies uniformly to all $2^{2j}$ self-dual Stratonovich–Weyl kernels.

STEP 1 · HALF-INTEGER SPINS

Antipodal Oddness on $S^2$

For $j \in \mathbb{N}_0 + \frac{1}{2}$, the operator $H(\mathbf{u}) = i e^{-i\pi\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{J}}$ has eigenvalues $\pm 1$ and satisfies $H(-\mathbf{u}) = -H(\mathbf{u})$. Thus $h(\mathbf{u}) = \langle\psi|H(\mathbf{u})|\psi\rangle$ is continuous, real, and odd on connected $S^2$, guaranteeing a zero $\mathbf{u}_*$ by the intermediate value theorem.

STEP 2 · INTEGER SPINS

Spherical Harmonic Zeros & Equatorial Average

A short divergence-theorem argument on simply connected $S^2$ shows every nonzero complex spherical harmonic of degree $j \ge 1$ vanishes at some $\mathbf{n}_* \in S^2$. Applied to $Y_\psi(\mathbf{n}) = \langle j,0;\mathbf{n}|\psi\rangle$, the equatorial integral identity forces the real expectation $\langle\psi|e^{-i\pi\mathbf{u}(\phi)\cdot\mathbf{J}}|\psi\rangle$ to have zero mean on the great circle orthogonal to $\mathbf{n}_*$, hence a zero $\mathbf{u}_* \perp \mathbf{n}_*$.

STEP 3 · ANALYTIC OBSTRUCTION

Traciality & Great-Circle Polynomiality

If $W_\psi \ge 0$ everywhere, traciality and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-covariance turn $|\langle\psi|D^{(j)}(g_*)|\psi\rangle|^2 = 0$ into $W_\psi(\mathbf{n})W_\psi(R_{g_*}^{-1}\mathbf{n}) \equiv 0$. Hence $W_\psi$ vanishes on a nonempty open set, so its restriction to every great circle through that set is a trigonometric polynomial with infinitely many roots, forcing $W_\psi \equiv 0$, contradicting $\int_{S^2} W_\psi\,d\mu = 1$.

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{-i\pi \mathbf{u}(\phi)\cdot\mathbf{J}}\,d\phi = (-1)^j\, |j, 0; \mathbf{n}\rangle\langle j, 0; \mathbf{n}| \qquad (\mathbf{u}(\phi) \perp \mathbf{n},\; j \in \mathbb{N})$$
